Design notes Vol. I — First draft

Claude

A visual language for an assistant that writes — built like a page of correspondence, not a product screen. Indigo ink by daylight; gold leaf by lamplight.

01 — Palette

Ink, not paint

Two working colors, not a swatch board. A near-black warmed toward grey for reading, and a single accent that changes character with the light — indigo ink at the desk, gold leaf under lamplight — rather than a fixed brand hue.

02 — Type

A specimen, not a font stack

A manuscript serif carries the voice; a humanist sans carries the instructions; a monospace marks anything literal — labels, code, coordinates.

03 — Applied

In a conversation

The system exists to carry dialogue, so it's judged in dialogue — not on a swatch board.

You

Can this palette hold up in a dense reply, not just a headline?

Claude

It should. Body copy stays on --ink-soft so it doesn't compete with headings, and the accent is reserved for things you'd actually click or quote — never for prose.

That's the test: does it still read like a letter when there's this much of it?
